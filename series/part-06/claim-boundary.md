# Claim boundary

## What Part 6 supports

In the tested cryptocurrency deliberation setting, across the homogeneous and heterogeneous fleets:

1. Consensus-following does not exceed chance at H = 1, H = 3, or H = 5.
2. The heterogeneous fleet exhibits substantially different deliberative form without improved consensus-following accuracy.
3. The tested data therefore support a **form–validity dissociation**: internal structure can change without a corresponding gain in external validity.

## What Part 6 does not support

Part 6 does **not** establish that:

- heterogeneity can never improve accuracy in another fleet, domain, or task;
- disagreement is intrinsically harmful or useless;
- no internal variable could ever predict external validity;
- the market task contains no forecastable signal;
- any particular confound identified in Part 5 explains the validity result;
- individual agents necessarily perform the same as consensus-following;
- another aggregation rule could not outperform the observed consensus rule;
- any intervention or controller is validated.

## Important design limitation

The fleets in Part 5 differ in more than lineage diversity alone. Therefore the observed change in deliberative form should be attributed conservatively to **fleet composition under the tested configuration**, not to pure heterogeneity as an isolated mechanism.

That limitation does not erase the Part 6 dissociation: whatever mixture of fleet properties caused the structural change, that structural change was not accompanied by improved consensus-following accuracy in the reported data.

## Public reading rule

**Different deliberation is not automatically better deliberation, and better-looking dynamics are not a substitute for external adjudication.**
