# Measurement Boundary

The three Part 2 observables answer questions about **deliberative form**:

| Observable | Question |
|---|---|
| λ∥ | Is an individual agent's trajectory stabilizing or changing? |
| λ⊥ | How separated are the agents in representation space? |
| τ | Does an agent's current representation align with peers' prior context? |

They do **not** directly answer:

- Is this claim factually true?
- Is the evidence reliable?
- Is the consensus externally correct?
- Are the agents sharing the same hallucination or training-data artifact?
- Did one agent causally copy another rather than respond to a common cause?

This is a deliberate measurement boundary, not an assumption that geometry equals truth.

A multi-agent system can display smooth convergence, high engagement, and stable trajectories while collectively reasoning toward a false conclusion. It can also display disagreement or instability while eventually producing a correct one.

The experimental question is therefore not whether these observables *define* truth. They do not.

The question is whether patterns in these internal observables contain **predictive information about an independently adjudicated external outcome**.

Part 1 defines that external-adjudication boundary. Part 2 defines the internal instruments. Keeping the two separate is necessary to test their relationship without building the desired conclusion into the measurement itself.
