# Consensus: What the Word Means Here

In the X series, **consensus** does not mean truth, confidence, or independent verification.

It refers to agreement among the agents on the directional decision being evaluated in the experiment.

That distinction is essential:

- **Consensus** describes the fleet's internal state or collective decision.
- **External validity** describes whether that recorded decision agrees with the later externally observed outcome.

These are intentionally kept separate.

A clean or stable consensus can therefore be externally wrong. Conversely, disagreement inside the fleet does not by itself imply that the reasoning is poor.

Part 1 begins from exactly this separation: if agreement and correctness are not the same variable, can the *dynamics leading to agreement* tell us anything useful about later external validity?

Later parts introduce the measurements used to characterize those dynamics and report the empirical results.
