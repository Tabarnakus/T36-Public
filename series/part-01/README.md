# X Series — Part 1: The Question

## Public claim

Part 1 asks a deliberately narrow question:

> When multiple AI agents reach consensus, can anything measurable inside their deliberation tell us whether that consensus will later be vindicated or refuted by an external outcome?

The cryptocurrency market is used here as an **external adjudicator**, not because T36 is a trading system and not because market direction is a universal definition of truth.

The experimental logic is simple:

1. Give a multi-agent fleet a bounded market observation window.
2. Record the agents' directional judgments and deliberative state before the future outcome is known.
3. Freeze that decision.
4. Observe the market at fixed future horizons.
5. Compare the recorded consensus with the later external outcome.

This preserves the distinction that matters for T36:

**internal agreement is an observation; external validity is adjudicated outside the fleet.**

## Scope

Part 1 does **not** claim that consensus predicts truth. That is the hypothesis being tested.

It also does not claim that short-horizon cryptocurrency markets generalize to every reasoning domain. They provide a bounded environment in which a decision can be recorded first and adjudicated later by an outcome the agents do not control.

Later parts of the series report what happened when the hypothesis was tested.
