# Claim boundary

## What Part 5 supports

Under six matched real-market windows, changing from the tested homogeneous fleet to the tested heterogeneous fleet increased native disagreement in every paired window, with a mean increase of 14.6 percentage points and a Wilcoxon signed-rank p-value of 0.031.

This supports a bounded causal claim:

> **Fleet composition affects deliberative form under held-constant market windows.**

## What Part 5 does not establish

Part 5 does **not** establish that:

- heterogeneity alone is the unique cause of the effect;
- any arbitrary heterogeneous fleet will behave similarly;
- greater disagreement is intrinsically healthier;
- greater disagreement implies better reasoning;
- greater disagreement implies better external accuracy;
- the healthy-regime interpretation from Part 4 has been proven;
- the synthetic calibration has been validated.

## Remaining confounds

The fleet intervention changes more than one latent property at once. The compared fleets differ in model family, vendor, lineage, scale/capability profile, and potentially interaction priors.

A stronger next experiment for mechanism identification would capability-match fleets while varying heterogeneity as independently as practical.

## Reading rule

**Changing who deliberates changed how the deliberation looked.**

That is the Part 5 result. Whether the changed form carries any external value is a separate question.
